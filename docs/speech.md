# Speaker script – Which free OS for 100 Asus PCs?

## Come usarlo

Un sistema a testa: Lino apre e presenta Linux Mint, Davide presenta Zorin OS Lite, Dennis Endless OS, e Roberto chiude con il confronto, la nostra scelta e il piano. Ognuno ha 3 minuti esatti: all'inizio premete **Play the tour** e il sito divide il tempo così.

| Chi | Tappe del tour |
| --- | --- |
| Lino | The mission, Their first computer, Inside the computer, Who we’re choosing for, About 100 in, 3 out, Linux Mint |
| Davide | Zorin OS Lite (il tour resta qui 3 minuti: c’è tempo per la demo del desktop) |
| Dennis | Endless OS (il tour resta qui 3 minuti: c’è tempo per la demo del desktop) |
| Roberto | How they score, Energy, Accessibility, Our pick, How to install it, From box to classroom, Questions? |

Il timer è nascosto nella chiavetta USB con gli occhi, sotto la barra in alto: in 3 minuti attraversa lo schermo da sinistra a destra.

- Linea arancione: tutto a posto.
- Linea rossa: mancano 30 secondi, chiudete.
- La chiavetta trema: mancano 10 secondi, ultima frase.
- A 3:00 il tour passa da solo al compagno successivo (se il desktop della demo è aperto, si chiude da solo) e la chiavetta riparte da sinistra. Dopo Roberto si ferma su Questions?.

**Demo del desktop (Davide e Dennis):** cliccate il pulsante con il monitor accanto al nome del sistema; il desktop si apre a tutto schermo e si usa davvero. Esc lo chiude. Mentre è aperto le frecce servono al desktop, non al tour.

**Telefoni del pubblico (prima di iniziare):** premete il pulsante con il telefono in alto (o J): compare un QR con un codice di sei lettere. La classe lo inquadra e ogni telefono diventa uno dei 100 PC, con il suo numero; i quadratini si accendono sullo schermo. Poi premete **Play the tour**: il riquadro va in un angolo e i telefoni seguono da soli le tappe. A **How to install it** i telefoni installano Endless OS dieci alla volta, come le dieci chiavette, e i quadratini si riempiono di arancione. Serve internet sul PC che presenta e sui telefoni; se la rete della scuola lo blocca, il sito va avanti normalmente. **End** chiude la stanza.

**Senza internet:** aprite il sito una volta con internet, sullo stesso PC e browser (anche il giorno prima): da lì funziona anche senza. Se il Wi-Fi cade, in alto compare l'adesivo **Offline** e si continua. Si può anche installare come app: in Chrome l'icona di installazione nella barra dell'indirizzo, in Safari File → Aggiungi al Dock.

Con il telecomando o le frecce → e ← andate avanti o indietro di una tappa: il tempo che vi resta si ridistribuisce sulle tappe rimaste. **Pause**, nella barra in basso, ferma il tour e anche il passaggio automatico; Esc lo chiude. Per una prova veloce, wedarealgoat.dev/?slot=60 fa durare ogni parte 60 secondi.

- Le righe **[Nome della tappa]** dicono dove si trova il tour; le righe in *corsivo tra parentesi* sono le azioni da fare.
- Non leggete parola per parola: imparate a memoria la prima e l’ultima frase e usate il resto come traccia.
- Le frasi in **grassetto** sono quelle chiave: rallentate e guardate il pubblico.
- Ogni parte è di 330–400 parole più la demo: a ritmo calmo dura circa 2:45, così vi restano secondi di margine.

## Speaker 1 – Lino Maini

*The mission and Linux Mint · 3 minutes*

**[The mission]**

Good morning, everyone. What you see is a box of one hundred computers taking off from Asus, in Taiwan. Asus is donating them to an NGO, and they are flying to schools where being online is very different from here.

Two billion people are still offline, and in low-income countries only 32 percent of people use the internet. Where these PCs are going, Wi-Fi and power come and go.

**Our job: pick the free system that works anyway.**

**[Their first computer]**

For many of these users, this will be their very first computer. I’m Lino, and together with Davide, Dennis and Roberto, I’m going to give you our answer.

**[Inside the computer]**

Inside, every computer is the same: parts, a chip, code and bits. What changes everything for the user is the system on top.

**[Who we’re choosing for]**

One hundred identical desktops, installed once and used for years, often far from any technician, by people with no Windows habits to fall back on. **So the best system is not the most powerful one. It’s the one people can’t get lost in, and that keeps working when the internet doesn’t.**

**[About 100 in, 3 out]**

We started from about one hundred free systems and asked four questions: is it really free and open, is it still updated, can a beginner use it, and is it the best of its kind? That ruled out ChromeOS Flex, which is closed, abandoned projects, expert systems like Arch, and good systems like Ubuntu that a finalist beat. **Three survived, and each of us will present one.**

**[Linux Mint]**

I’ll start with the familiar one: Linux Mint.

Look at the screen: a menu in the corner, a taskbar at the bottom, windows with the usual buttons. It looks a lot like Windows, and that’s the point: if these users later work in an office, what they learned will still be useful.

Mint is stable and well tested, and it has one of the biggest communities in the Linux world, so help is easy to find. It needs 2 gigabytes of memory, 4 recommended, and each version gets updates for about five years.

**The weak point: Mint expects an internet connection to install apps and updates.** Now Davide will show you the light one.

## Speaker 2 – Davide Miron

*Zorin OS Lite · 3 minutes · the tour stays on Zorin OS Lite*

**[Zorin OS Lite]**

Thank you, Lino. I’m Davide, and my system is the light one: Zorin OS Lite.

Zorin OS Lite is built on Xfce, one of the lightest desktops around. A lighter desktop asks less from the processor and the memory, so it stays quick on modest computers and uses a bit less electricity. In places where power is a real problem, that matters.

It also looks familiar: a simple start menu and a taskbar, much like the Windows computers you know.

*(Click the monitor button next to “Zorin OS Lite”: the desktop opens.)*

This is a simplified copy of the Zorin desktop that we built into our website, and it really works. Here is the menu: I can search for an app, open the files, write a note and save it. Windows open, move and close just like on a real computer. And look at the Wi-Fi icon: it’s offline, like in many of the schools we’re talking about.

*(Press Esc to close the desktop.)*

But our research found an important detail. **Zorin says version 18 is the last one with a Lite edition.** It’s supported until June 2029; after that, users would have to move to a heavier edition. Its community is also smaller than Mint’s and, like Mint, it needs the internet for most apps.

And to be honest about energy: all three systems run on 2 gigabytes of memory, and a new Asus desktop handles any of them easily. The biggest savings come from settings like sleep mode and screen dimming, which all three have.

**So Zorin Lite wins on energy, but it has the shortest future.** Dennis will show you the offline one.

## Speaker 3 – Dennis Boschi

*Endless OS · 3 minutes · the tour stays on Endless OS*

**[Endless OS]**

Thanks, Davide. I’m Dennis, and my system is the offline one: Endless OS.

Endless was designed for exactly the people we’re talking about: homes and schools with little or no internet. It comes with an encyclopedia, learning content and many apps already inside, so the computer is useful from the very first day, without any connection.

*(Click the monitor button next to “Endless OS”: the desktop opens.)*

This is our working copy of the Endless desktop. The home screen is a grid of icons, like a smartphone, which many people already know. I type “solar” in the search bar and press Enter: the encyclopedia opens the article, with no internet at all. If I open the browser, it says we’re offline and offers the encyclopedia instead.

*(Press Esc to close the desktop.)*

There’s another advantage: users can’t change the core system, so it’s very hard to break by accident. If an update fails, the previous version is still there. Updates and apps can even be copied from one PC to another with a USB stick.

It also helps people with different needs: zoom, large text and high-contrast themes, the Orca screen reader, which reads the screen aloud, and an on-screen keyboard. It’s available in dozens of languages, including right-to-left scripts like Arabic.

**The downsides: the full version is a big download, it looks less like an office PC, and apps come only from its own App Center.**

So, three good options. Which one is best? Roberto will compare them and give you our answer.

## Speaker 4 – Roberto Cacace

*The comparison, our pick and the plan · 3 minutes*

**[How they score]**

Thank you, Dennis. I’m Roberto. To compare the three, we scored each one from one to five on five criteria, using the official documentation and reviews. Endless gets a five for being easy on day one and a five for working without internet; Mint is the safest for future updates.

**Adding it up: Endless 20 out of 25, Mint 18, Zorin Lite 16.**

**[Energy]**

On energy, Zorin Lite wins, but on a new PC the difference is small.

**[Accessibility]**

On accessibility, all three are good, and Mint and Endless make the settings easiest to find.

**[Our pick]**

**So our recommendation for Asus is Endless OS.** There are three reasons. First, it’s useful on day one with no internet at all: the encyclopedia, the learning content and the apps are already on the computer. Second, it’s hard to break: users can’t damage the core system by accident, and the previous version is kept as a backup. Third, it was made for exactly these users.

Our plan B: where the internet is reliable and people need office skills, Linux Mint is an excellent choice.

**[How to install it]**

*(If the class joined with their phones, point at the squares in the corner: their phones are installing now, ten at a time.)*

Here is how we would install it on one hundred PCs. We download the full edition of Endless OS and turn a USB stick of at least 32 gigabytes into a “reformatter” stick. On each Asus PC, we hold F2 and press power to open the BIOS, press F8 for the Boot Menu and pick the stick. The stick copies Endless OS onto the PC, and we move on to the next one.

**With ten sticks, ten PCs install at once: ten rounds, and all one hundred are ready.**

**[From box to classroom]**

Then we train five to ten local people as computer champions: they learn first, then they help everyone else. One PC with internet downloads the updates, and a USB stick carries them to the others. And the software cost for all one hundred computers? Zero.

**[Questions?]**

To sum up: **a computer should keep working even when the internet doesn’t.** Asus isn’t just giving away one hundred machines: with the right system, it’s giving one hundred communities a first window onto the world’s knowledge.

Thank you for listening. We’re happy to answer your questions.

## Likely questions

Short answers anyone in the group can give.

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why not Windows? | It isn't free: 100 licences cost money, and it needs regular internet for updates. The brief also asked for a free system. |
| Why not Ubuntu? | Ubuntu is good, but Linux Mint is built on it and has a more familiar layout, so for beginners Mint does the same job better. |
| Won't they need Windows skills for jobs? | The basics (mouse, keyboard, files, browser) are the same everywhere. Where office skills matter and the internet is good, our plan B is Mint. |
| Can Endless open Word or Excel files? | Yes, with a free office app such as LibreOffice from its App Center. |
| How do you add apps without internet? | Many are already installed. Others can be copied from another Endless PC with a USB stick. |
| Is Endless OS still alive? | Yes: version 6 got updates in 2026, and Endless OS 7 is being built. |
| What about viruses? | Viruses for Linux are rare, and the Endless core system is read-only, so it is very hard to damage. |
| Why not Zorin OS Lite, if it's the lightest? | Its Lite edition ends after 2029, it needs internet for most apps, and on new Asus PCs the energy difference is small. |
